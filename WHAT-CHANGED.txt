The account page no longer promises a shop that isn't open
==========================================================

Upload:

  account/index.html

Nothing else changed. account/app.js is untouched.


THE TWO UNTRUE LINES
--------------------
Before:
  "Create an account to buy from the masjid shop and keep track of your orders."
  "You are signed in. Your orders will be saved to this account."

After:
  "The masjid shop isn't open yet, so there is nothing to buy today. Create an
   account now and it will be ready the moment it opens."
  "You're signed in. The shop isn't open yet - when it is, this is the account
   it will use."

The gold button said "Go to the shop" and led to a page that says the shop is on
its way. It now says "See what's coming to the shop", which is what the page
actually shows.

That is the whole of the honesty fix, and it took ten minutes. It is the same
call you made twice already - on Gift Aid, and on the "100% of your donation"
line - and it was the last thing on the live site making a claim the masjid
cannot currently keep.


SOMETHING ELSE I FOUND WHILE IN THERE
-------------------------------------
The register form asks for a full name, an email address and a password, and
said nothing at all about what happens to them. No purpose, no link to the
privacy notice, no consent line.

Every other form on this site has one. Hall hire, nikah, admissions and the
course sign-ups all state what they collect and make people tick a box. This
one - the only form on the site that creates a permanent record of a person -
had nothing.

Under UK GDPR Article 13 you have to tell people what you are collecting and
why, at the point you collect it. So the form now carries:

  "We store your name and email address so that you can sign in, and for
   nothing else. We do not share them, and there are no cookies or tracking on
   this site. Privacy notice."

with a working link through to the privacy page. I checked the link resolves
rather than assuming it.

I did NOT add a tick-box. For a straightforward account sign-up the lawful basis
is contract rather than consent, and a box that everyone ticks without reading
adds friction without adding protection. If your DPIA work concludes otherwise,
it is a five-minute change.


ONE THING FOR YOU AND THE COMMITTEE TO DECIDE
---------------------------------------------
The copy is now honest. The underlying question is not a copy question:

  Should the public be invited to register at all, while there is nothing for
  an account to do?

The Register and Login buttons sit in the site header on every page. Someone who
follows them gets a truthful page telling them there is nothing to buy yet. That
is fine. But you are still collecting names and email addresses for a service
that does not exist, and:

  * the list ages - people who sign up now may not remember doing so by the
    time the shop opens, and a "welcome back" email to a stranger is how
    complaints start;
  * you have to keep and protect those records in the meantime;
  * the privacy notice does not yet describe visitor accounts at all, which is
    worth closing whichever way you decide.

Two reasonable options:

  A. Leave it as it is. The page tells the truth, and a small list of early
     accounts is genuinely useful on the day the shop opens.

  B. Take Register and Login out of the public header until the shop exists,
     and leave the account page reachable by its address. Staff keep their
     route in; the public are not asked for data nobody can yet use.

I would pick B if the shop is more than a couple of months away, and A if it is
close. That is a judgement about the shop's timetable, which you have and I do
not - so I have changed nothing there.


TESTED
------
A browser test asserts the word "orders" no longer appears anywhere on the page,
that both cards say the shop is not open, that the button no longer calls a
coming-soon page "the shop", that the register form names what it collects and
links to the privacy notice, and that the link actually opens the privacy page
on the main site. The layout was re-checked at three widths.
