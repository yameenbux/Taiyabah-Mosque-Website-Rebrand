Madrasah Privacy Notice

What we keep about your child, and about our staff. Articles 13 and 14, UK General Data Protection Regulation.

This notice changed on 27 September 2026, and it matters

The madrasah has started keeping a register, and teachers now have their own logins. We said in earlier versions that we would tell you before the first register mark was made and before any teacher had an account, rather than afterwards. This is us doing both.

A teacher sees the classes they teach and nothing else — not another class, not your address, not the fees. What they can see, and why they can see the medical information, is set out under “Who can see it” below.

The previous version said the madrasah kept your child’s name, class and dates only, and held no date of birth, address, telephone number or medical information. That is no longer true, and this version says what is actually held.

Nothing was taken without being given: all of it came from the madrasah’s own previous records when they were moved into the new system in September 2026. What went wrong is that the notice was not updated at the same time. We are telling you rather than replacing the page quietly.

In one paragraph

We keep your child’s name, date of birth, class, whether they are a boy or a girl, the dates they joined and left, and your family’s address. For a small number of children we also keep medical, allergy or additional-needs information that a parent told us, so that staff can look after them safely. We keep your name and a way of reaching you, and what the family has been charged and paid.

We do not share any of it with anybody outside the masjid. We keep it for three years after your child leaves and then it is deleted automatically.

The rest of this notice explains that properly, tells you what you can ask us to do, and tells you how to complain if you are not happy.

Who we are, and who to ask

The madrasah is run by Bolton Central Islamic Society, a registered charity (number 1041569), which operates Taiyabah Masjid in Bolton. In data protection language the charity is the controller for the records described here — it is the organisation responsible for them, and the organisation you can hold to account.

Ask at the masjid office and you will be put in touch with the right person, or write to info@taiyabahmasjid.com marking it for the Data Protection Lead. You do not need to put it in writing, though it helps us if you do.

We reply within one month. If your request is complicated we may take up to two more months, and we will tell you within the first month if that is going to happen. It costs nothing, and we will not ask why.

Most children never have one. It records what happened, when, and who wrote it — not an opinion about your family.

What we do not hold about your child

  • A photograph of your child
  • Their nationality, ethnicity or first language
  • Behaviour or merit marks
  • Test or examination results
  • Anything about your child's progress or ability

None of this is missing by accident. Each was considered and left out, because the honest question — “what would the madrasah actually do with it?” — had no good answer. Information we never collect cannot be lost, leaked or misused.

We are building an attendance register. When it starts being used we will issue a new version of this notice and tell you before the first mark is made, not afterwards.

Where we got it

From you, when you enrolled your child, and from the madrasah’s own previous record system, which these records replaced in September 2026. We did not obtain anything about your child from anywhere else, and we did not add anything to it.

Why we hold it, and what allows us to

We hold it to run the madrasah: to know which children are enrolled, which class each child is in, which teacher is responsible for them, how to reach their family, and what any of them needs in order to be safe here.

Our lawful basis Legitimate interests — Article 6(1)(f). Our interest is running a madrasah the community asked us to run, and keeping the children in it safe. We have weighed that against your child’s privacy.
Why not consent Because consent has to be freely given, and it would not be: if you could not refuse without your child losing their place, it is not a real choice. Calling it consent would be dishonest.
Being on a madrasah roll says something about religion The law treats that as needing extra protection (Article 9). We rely on Article 9(2)(d), which allows a not-for-profit religious body to keep records about its own members and the people who come to it regularly — provided it does not share them outside the organisation without consent. We do not share them.
Medical, allergy and additional-needs information This is health information, which the law protects separately (Article 9). We rely on Article 9(2)(d) — records kept by a not-for-profit religious body about the children who come to it, not disclosed outside without consent — and, where a child needs help urgently, on Article 9(2)(c), protecting their vital interests. We hold it only where a parent has told us, only what they told us, and it is never included in any list, export or printed register.

Because our basis is legitimate interests, you have the right to object to it. That is a real right and not a formality — see “What you can ask us to do”.

Do you have to give it to us?

There is no law that says you must. We cannot enrol a child without a name, a date of birth and a class, and we cannot run a madrasah safely with no way of reaching anybody when a child is unwell.

The medical information is different. You do not have to tell us about an allergy, a condition or an additional need, and your child will not lose their place if you do not. We ask because a member of staff who does not know cannot act, and because the alternative is finding out during the emergency. If you have told us something and want it removed, say so and we will remove it.

Who can see it

  • Administrators at the masjid, and nobody else. Today that is three people.
  • Every one of them has to use a second step to sign in — a changing code from an app on their phone, as well as a password. A stolen password on its own is not enough to see anything.
  • Lists do not show the sensitive things. A screen listing the children shows a small mark meaning a child has a medical note — not what the note says. Somebody has to open that one child’s record deliberately to read it, and the system writes down who did and when. The same is true of your telephone number and your address.
  • Teachers now have accounts, and see only their own classes. The last version of this notice said no teacher had one and promised you would be told before that changed. This is us doing that. A teacher signs in and can see: the classes they teach, the children in those classes, whether each child came in, any medical, allergy or additional-needs information for those children, and one telephone number to ring. Nothing else. Not another class, not your address, not the fees, not the roll as a whole. That is not a rule the screen follows — the database refuses to answer a teacher who asks for anything more.
  • Why a teacher can see the medical information. Because that is why you told us. A teacher who does not know a child carries an inhaler cannot act, and the alternative is finding out during the emergency. Every time a teacher opens a child’s record it is written down with their name against it.
  • Nobody outside the masjid sees them. We do not share them with any other mosque, school, council, charity or company. We do not sell them. We do not use them to ask you for money.

The one exception we must be honest about: if we ever had a serious concern about a child’s safety, we would share what was necessary with the authorities whose job that is. That is a different situation with its own legal basis, and we would not need your permission for it. It has nothing to do with the ordinary running of the madrasah.

Safeguarding records work differently, and you should know how

A concern raised about a child is read by the safeguarding lead and nobody else — not by the teacher who raised it, once it is sent, and not by other staff.

If you ask to see what we hold about your child, we may not be able to show you a safeguarding record, and we may not always be able to tell you one exists. The law allows that where showing it would put a child at risk. We are telling you the rule now rather than using it as a surprise later. If it ever applies to you we will say that we are withholding something and why, as far as we are able.

The system we used before

Until September 2026 these records were kept in a different system, and that copy still exists. The masjid remains responsible for it while it does. We are arranging for those records to be deleted at source, and until that is done everything in this notice — your rights included — applies to that copy as well as to this one. If you ask us to delete your child’s record, we will delete it from both.

Where it is kept

In a secure database hosted in London, in the United Kingdom. The company that provides the hosting is based in the United States and its support staff could in principle need access to fix a fault, which is covered by a written contract requiring them to protect the data to UK standards and to act only on our instructions.

How long we keep it

Three years after your child leaves the madrasah. Then it is deleted permanently, by an automatic process that runs every week. We do not have to remember to do it, and we cannot quietly forget.

Three years is long enough to answer a question raised after the event, or to welcome a family back without starting again, and short enough that we are not keeping a permanent list of who attended a madrasah. If we remove a record by mistake it goes to an archive we can restore it from, and the same three-year clock applies to that copy.

What you can ask us to do

These are your rights in law. You can use any of them by asking the Data Protection Lead. We will not charge you and we will not ask why.

Show you what we holdWe will give you a copy of your child's record. It is longer than it once was — the list above — so allow us a few days rather than a few minutes.
Correct somethingIf a name is spelled wrong, a date of birth is wrong or a class is wrong, tell us and we will change it straight away. Some dates of birth came across from the old system looking doubtful and we would be glad to be corrected.
Delete itIf your child has left, we will normally agree. While your child is attending we may need to keep the record in order to teach them safely; if we say no, we will explain why in writing.
Stop using itYou can ask us to pause while a disagreement is sorted out. We will keep the record but not use it.
Object to us holding itYou can object at any time. We then have to stop unless we can show compelling reasons that outweigh your child's interests. We will answer in writing and give our reasons.
Take the medical information back off usIf you told us about an allergy or a condition and you would rather we did not keep it written down, say so. We will talk to you about what that means for looking after your child, and we will do as you ask.
Take it elsewhereThe right to data portability does not apply here, because it only covers information held with consent or under a contract. We have said so plainly rather than leave you to find out when you ask.

Why we may ask which class your child is in

Some children at the madrasah share a name — twelve names on our roll belong to two different children each. So if you ask us about your child, we will ask which class they are in before we show you anything or change anything. It is not obstruction: it is how we make sure we do not show you another family’s record, or delete the wrong child’s.

If your child is old enough to understand the request, we will take their view into account before showing their record to anyone, including you. The rights belong to the child; a parent exercises them on their behalf while the child is too young to do it themselves.

Decisions made by computer

There are none. Nothing in our system scores, ranks, sorts or decides anything about a child. Every decision about your child is made by a person.

Why we hold it, and what allows us to

WhyTo tell you what the madrasah fees are, to take them, to keep the charity’s own record of what was charged and received, and to be able to reach somebody if your child is unwell.
What allows us toArticle 6(1)(f) — our legitimate interests. The madrasah is paid for by these fees, and we cannot ask for them, or tell you your child has been hurt, without a way of reaching you.
Is this special category data?No. It is ordinary personal data about an adult. The special rules in Part A apply to your child’s record, not to yours.

About the reminders we send

  • One of you, not both. We write to one nominated adult per family. If you would rather it were the other parent, tell the office.
  • It never names your child. A reminder says the family, the reference and the amount. An email can be forwarded or read by somebody else, and what your child attends is nobody else’s business.
  • Not more than once a week, and never if the balance is already clear when we press send.
  • You can ask us to stop emailing you and to ring you instead. That is an objection under Article 21 and we will honour it. We cannot stop telling you what is owed — but we can change how.

If money is difficult

Tell the office. The madrasah reduces and waives fees for families in hardship, and it would far rather have that conversation than have a child stop coming. Nothing about asking is recorded against your child.

How long we keep it

Your details are deleted when the family’s record is — three years after the last child in the family has left. The record of money charged and received is kept for six years, because that is how long a charity must keep its accounts. That is a legal requirement on us and not a choice.

DBS — what we hold and what we do not

We record that a check was done and when. Nothing more. No certificate number, no copy of the disclosure, and no note of any conviction, caution or offence.

  • Why the certificate number is not held: it does not prove a check was clear — the date and the Update Service status do that — and its main use to somebody else would be pretending to be you.
  • Why we do not record outcomes: information about convictions and offences is tightly controlled by law and needs its own written justification and policy. We have not taken that on, so we do not hold it. If that ever changes we will tell you first.

We also do not hold your National Insurance number, your bank details, or your gender.

Why we hold it, and what allows us to

If you are paid or under an agreement with us Article 6(1)(b) — we need it to perform that agreement.
If you volunteerArticle 6(1)(f), legitimate interests — running the madrasah and keeping children safe. We need to be able to identify and contact the adults we put in front of children.
Is this special category data?No. Your record is ordinary personal data about an adult.

Who can see it, and for how long

  • Masjid administrators only, under the same two-step sign-in described in Part A.
  • Screens that list all staff do not show your address, date of birth or hours. They show a small mark meaning “on file” or “not on file”. Somebody has to open your record deliberately to see the details, and that is recorded.
  • Kept for three years after you leave, then deleted, the same as a pupil record.

You have the same rights as those in Part A, and you exercise them the same way.

Part C — complaints, security, and changes

If you are not happy

Please come to us first. Most things are a misunderstanding or a mistake we can put right the same day, and we would rather fix it than have you go elsewhere to get it fixed.

You do not have to, though, and you can complain to the Information Commissioner at any time. Complaining to them costs nothing and does not affect your child’s place at the madrasah in any way.

Helpline0303 123 1113 — they will talk you through it
Online ico.org.uk/make-a-complaint
By postThe ICO moved offices during 2026. Rather than print an address that may be out of date, please take the current one from ico.org.uk or ask them on the helpline.

How we keep it safe

  • Two-step sign-in for every administrator, required by the system itself and not just by the page.
  • The records cannot be read directly even by somebody inside the system; every route to them checks who is asking first.
  • A list can never carry a medical note, an allergy or a SEND record. That is enforced in the database and checked automatically, not left to whoever writes the next screen to remember.
  • Who looked at what, and when, is recorded.
  • Information travels encrypted, and is stored encrypted.
  • Deletion happens automatically on a schedule, so it does not depend on anybody remembering.

If something does go wrong we have a written procedure for it. If a breach is likely to put you or your child at serious risk, we will tell you directly and tell you what to do. Where the law requires it we will also report it to the Information Commissioner within 72 hours.

Where this notice lives, and how you were told about it

This notice is published at taiyabahmasjid.com/madrasah-privacy and linked from the footer of every page. It is not handed out on paper as a matter of course, but ask at the office and you will be given a printed copy — you should not need a computer to find out what is held about your child.

We will have told you it exists. Putting a notice on a website and waiting to be found is not telling anybody anything, and the law asks us to take an active step rather than a passive one. So when this notice is published, and whenever it materially changes, the masjid writes to parents once to say so and records the date it did.

There is a second, separate notice covering the website itself — hall hire, nikāḥ requests, adult classes and the shop. It is at taiyabahmasjid.com/#privacy and it does not cover the madrasah. This one does.

Changes to this notice

If we start holding something new, share records with somebody new, or keep them for longer, we will issue a new version of this notice and tell you before the change takes effect — not afterwards, and not by quietly replacing the page and hoping you look. The version number and date are at the top.

What this notice does not cover

This notice is about the madrasah’s records only. It does not cover the masjid’s website, donations and Gift Aid, hall hire, nikāḥ bookings, or the masjid’s mobile app. Those are separate and have their own arrangements.
