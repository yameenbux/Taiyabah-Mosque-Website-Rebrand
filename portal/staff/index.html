Who they are

The title is kept apart from the name on purpose. In the old system “Apa” was part of the name, so an A to Z list put nineteen people under A. Here the list sorts by surname, which is how anybody actually looks somebody up.

This decides which of the two lists they appear in. Leave it as “Not set” if you genuinely do not know. A guess puts somebody in the wrong list and looks exactly like a fact, whereas anybody with no side chosen is shown in their own group at the top of the list until somebody who knows picks one. The import worked it out from the title, so the few it could not place are the ones with no title at all.

Somebody who has left keeps their record. Set their employment to Left and put the date here, and they drop out of the list and out of the DBS figures — without the madrasah losing what it held about who taught what and when.

How to reach them

The note shows on the row in the list, and it is yours — nothing else writes to it. Records brought over from the madrasah’s previous system used to arrive with a sentence already sitting here about their DBS; that has been moved out into its own field, so this box starts empty on every record and typing in it cannot destroy anything.

DBS

A DBS certificate has no expiry date, so the portal works out when a check falls due from the masjid’s own renewal period of 36 months from the date on the certificate. Somebody on the DBS Update Service should be re-checked every 12 months instead, counted from the date last checked — a check can be revoked the week after it is issued, so on the Update Service it is the looking that matters, not the certificate’s age.

The certificate number is not kept anywhere. It is the one thing here that would genuinely harm somebody if it leaked and the one thing this screen has no use for — what the masjid needs to know is whether the check is in date, and the certificate itself is in the office.
